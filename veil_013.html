“Delinquency is not a kind of natural regression of crime; it is the result of a tactical polyvalence of discourses, institutions, legal and illegal practices.” Michel Foucault — Discipline and Punish
Writer's Statement

Ghetto Suburbs isn’t a heist film — it’s a modern Greek tragedy, set not in ancient Athens but in the cracked concrete and warped dreams of America’s overlooked neighborhoods, post-COVID.

We follow three men — Mani, JB, and Dante — bound by very human, very urgent moral and financial quandaries. Their plan: rob their old high school. On the surface, it’s the theft of cameras — but underneath, it’s an act of hope and desperation.

What follows is not spectacle, but consequence — a consequence they’ve been conditioned to bear: the weight of institutional abandonment and the unlivable demands of survival.

This is a film of social critique and ethical tension — a cinematic case study of what happens when young men are born into a story already written, one that punishes deviation even when the script is suffocating.

With a visceral undercurrent of class commentary, Ghetto Suburbs asks not just what happens when you resist your fate — but what happens when that resistance is the only act of love you have left.

Creator

Joshua Odiase

Odyssy.

This is a draft — heavily edited, but still a draft. I still want to do more research in some areas to make it factually accurate, as well as keep working on the formatting and a few plot points.

I wrote this between December 2022 and summer 2025, from ages 17 to 19. It took a lot of inspiration from my hometown, Antioch, California (the YOC). I built the story around real places and one real event: the heist of our film (video production) class’s cameras, caught on CCTV. Yeah, niggas were mad about the equipment being stolen, but I was like, that’s a smart hit. I was also learning to work with my budget, inspired by great auteurs who used their own surroundings and peers as the basis for great storytelling, even without high-end money behind them.

For more context, I’ve had such an ambivalent relationship with this script. I wrote it on and (mostly) off during that period; sometimes I just wanted to table it and leave it, but I was set on finishing it. After graduating, I asked my old film teacher (who I didn’t view fondly back when I was his student) for his thoughts, since I wanted to make this a reality and put it into production at our school. He wanted to “go straight to the action,” as he said verbatim, and cut out basically the whole essence of the film. When I asserted that that wasn’t the point, my style, or the heart of the narrative, he left me on read. I already had mixed feelings about him, but it was still disheartening, especially as the student I believed pushed the envelope most in that class when it came to creating. Mind you, I made a whole 220-slide storyboard for this, edited it heavily, had people I trusted review it, and was even in talks with a producer from St. Louis to help with financing and budgeting (which was basically nonexistent). I was planning to pay out of my own pocket.

All to say, I let that one opinion get to me. From where I stand now, I couldn’t tell you why, but back then I was a different person. I used to let others limit what I could do and my own self-efficacy — how much I believed in myself. And even though I’d say I’ve outgrown this part of my story, with my style, interests, and world growing far beyond my hometown, this still deserves its light. Hope y’all enjoy — and remember, fuck what they say.

To anyone reading this, whether you know me or not: I always welcome opinions, feedback, or any thoughts. Just reach out through the contact form.
