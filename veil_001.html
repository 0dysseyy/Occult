O C C U L T

O C C U L T, derived from the Latin root occultus, meaning "hidden" or "concealed." The word Occult has often been symbolized through the eye within the triangle — the eye representing the singularity of perception, awareness, and consciousness, while the triangle represents the trinity between thought, feeling, and action. Together, they embody the unseen connection between what is known and unknown, the internal and external, the hidden and revealed.

O C C U L T is often taken in the sense of cabals and secret societies, but that's not where its root terms come from. The semantics of the word have taken on that meaning as language evolved into what the populace has been led to identify it as. O C C U L T exists in response to the disregard of feeling in our work, art, and lives — a disregard that brings disharmony. There was a time when the interior life was the primary texture of existence. People carried their own grittiness, their losses, their quiet failures, the particular weight of their particular lives, without anywhere to put it. The self was inescapable, and in that inescapability, paradoxically, it was whole. You could dissolve into this other medium — a medium that was for expression regardless of the imperfections of its beholder, not blind to their flaws but accepting of them. There are a lot of people who create — thinkers, writers, artists, filmmakers, musicians — who just don't put out work because of the fear of judgment, even though the medium is for escape: taking the internal and applying it to a canvas, an expression regardless of what those emotions make.

We have seen the artifice and nullity of work that is technically sound and appealing, but we know that a lot of individualized work isn't that ubiquitous and might not be as pretty compared to other pieces, given the source material they are creating from. Media no longer needs us to escape life. It needs to return us to it.

We see facades arrive faster than they can be read — a guise so enthralling, so cunning in its operation, that it takes us under its veil before we have located, through a lexicon, what is happening to us. We align with German philosopher Theodor Adorno's premise of how mainstream media corporatizes the human experience in music, film, and art, making beings docile bodies in the aesthetic realm. French philosopher Guy Debord touches on this in The Society of the Spectacle, describing how we have been pacified by these representations of connection and opulence. Appearances mediate their existence, hollowing out the reality of the complex human experience.

Media no longer needs us to escape life. It needs to return us to it.

O C C U L T now enters the labyrinth — not at the end, but somewhere in the interior, following a thread. Not with the certainty of this manifesto, which would require knowing where the corridor leads. We are still learning the dimensions of the space. What we know is that there are rooms in this labyrinth where a lot of work can't live and bloom. Corners of culture, thought, and aesthetic experience that require a different pace, a different quality of attention, a willingness to remain inside long enough for a piece to take shape.

We know that there are writers, filmmakers, artists, thinkers — people with craft in any capacity — doing work who want a reason to develop that craft, regardless of who they are, and to be able to make it and display it.

In this corridor, we want to move through those rooms slowly. To publish essays, films, anonymous accounts, poetry, criticism — work that trusts its reader to sustain attention, to remain inside an argument long enough for it to open. To carry what is hidden back into the light, or deeper into the dark, according to what it requires.

We do not know where this leads. The nature of a labyrinth is that you cannot see its shape from inside it. We are not supposed to know yet.

But consider: if you wanted a reason to pursue your art irrespective of the acclaim it would receive, but for "becoming," for something to express what you would rather not say, to put a stroke to a canvas that won't be perfect and be fine with it — then you have already been asking the question this collective exists to pursue.

You have been inside the veil longer than you knew.
